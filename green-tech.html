Green Technology

Explore three important renewable energy sources: solar, wind, and hydropower. Each one uses a different natural resource to generate electricity.

Renewable Energy Sources

Renewable energy technologies can help reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Their performance depends on location, weather, design, cost, and environmental planning.

Solar Energy

Solar panels use photovoltaic cells to convert sunlight directly into electricity. They can be installed on rooftops or in large solar farms.

Wind Energy

Wind turbines use moving air to turn their blades. The rotating shaft drives a generator that produces electricity.

Hydropower

Hydropower uses moving or falling water to spin turbines connected to generators. Some systems use dams, while others use the natural flow of rivers.

Renewable Energy Comparison

Use this table to compare how each technology works, its benefits, and some considerations before it is developed.

Comparison of Solar, Wind, and Hydropower
Energy Source How It Works Advantages Considerations
Solar Panels convert sunlight into electricity. Can be installed on rooftops; produces electricity without direct air pollution during operation. Output changes with sunlight and weather; panels need space and materials.
Wind Turbine blades turn a generator using wind. Produces electricity without burning fuel during operation; suitable for windy locations. Wind is variable; projects need careful site and wildlife planning.
Hydropower Flowing or falling water turns a turbine. Can provide steady electricity where water resources are suitable; some facilities can store energy. Dams and river projects can affect habitats, fish movement, and nearby communities.

Remember: No energy source is impact-free. Good planning considers local ecosystems, communities, cost, and long-term maintenance.
